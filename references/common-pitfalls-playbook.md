# Common Pitfalls Playbook

A quick diagnostic reference for the patterns that show up most often in a stalled or messy partner program. Use this for a fast read before deciding whether the situation needs a full Blueprint audit (see `partner-ecosystem-builder`) or a smaller, specific fix.

**"We recruited a lot of partners but almost none of them are producing."**
This is a recruitment-without-screening problem, almost always traceable to skipping or ignoring the Ideal Partner Profile. The fix isn't recruiting more partners, it's applying the IPP retroactively to the existing list, being honest about which partners were never going to produce, and redirecting the energy currently spent maintaining dead relationships toward the ones with real signal. Ten active partners beat fifty dormant ones on every metric that matters.

**"Sales and partners keep ending up on the same accounts."**
This is almost never a people problem, it's a missing or unenforced conflict resolution rule. Check whether a deal registration process exists at all, and if it does, check whether anyone actually follows it under pressure. The fix is naming the rule clearly (see the schema reference) and holding to it even when a specific deal makes the exception tempting, because the first exception is what teaches everyone the rule doesn't really apply.

**"Partners don't seem to understand the product well enough to sell it."**
This is an enablement gap, and it's the most common single cause of underperformance across every partner type. Check certification completion rate and how recently the sales briefs or battle cards were updated (see the enablement reference in `partner-ecosystem-builder`). Partners need more structured support than most programs budget for, and assuming they'll figure it out through osmosis is a common and expensive mistake.

**"We can't say with any confidence how much revenue partners actually produced."**
This is a schema and discipline problem, not a partner problem. Check whether the CRM fields in `ideal-partner-profile-and-crm-schema.md` exist at all, and if they do, check whether reps actually tag deals at the point of origin or reconstruct attribution later from memory. Retroactive tagging is unreliable and erodes trust between the partner team and sales. The fix is enforcing tagging in the existing deal review cadence, not building a new report.

**"We invest the same amount of time in every partner and get wildly uneven results."**
This is a tiering failure, either no tiers exist or the tiers exist on paper without real criteria behind them. Pull whatever activity data exists, rank partners by actual contribution, and set tier thresholds that reflect that ranking rather than tenure or who's easiest to reach. The top handful of partners should get materially more attention than everyone else, on purpose.

**"Partners keep bringing us leads that don't fit what we sell."**
This means the IPP or the sales briefs never made it into the partner's hands in a form they'd actually use, or the deal registration process doesn't reject bad-fit leads early. Define a clear qualification bar for partner-registered deals and hold to it. It's a harder conversation than accepting the lead and letting it die quietly in the pipeline, but it protects both sides' time.

**"This program hasn't produced meaningful ROI yet and people are losing patience."**
Partner programs are a long game almost everywhere: ninety to a hundred and twenty days to a first deal registration is normal for an early program, and meaningful revenue usually takes six to twelve months to show up even when the mechanics are sound. Check the leading indicators (registrations, pipeline created, activation rate) before concluding the program itself is broken. If leading indicators are also flat, that's a real signal. If they're healthy and only revenue lags, that's patience, not failure.

**"We built a portal and nobody uses it."**
Check what's actually in it against what partners are asking for in support requests or Slack messages. A portal that duplicates a partner's onboarding call instead of replacing the questions that come up after onboarding usually goes unused. Rebuild the content plan around the questions partners actually ask, not the questions the program assumed they'd have.
